# DB Panel Designer — v2.14.0

Phone-first designer that goes **floor plan → wiring → panel**: draw the rooms, fill them with switches, sockets, lights and fans (with a 3D view), then build the DB panel the circuits come from (DIN rails, breakers, busbars, wiring). Vanilla JS. Saves on the device and works offline; sign in (optional) to keep your projects in the cloud and open them from any phone or browser.

## Deploy
Hosted on **Cloudflare Workers** (Static Assets + a small API Worker) from the GitHub repo `nafaah14/DB-Panel-Designer`: every push to `main` is built and published automatically (config in `wrangler.jsonc`; `.assetsignore` keeps `node_modules`, `tools`, `worker` and other non-site files out of the upload). The cloud API in `worker/` stores each account in its own SQLite-backed Durable Object — the deploy creates it, there is nothing to set up in the dashboard. Optional: add an `INVITE_CODE` variable (Workers → Settings → Variables) to require a code when creating an account. For a quick phone test without hosting, open `dist/panel-designer-standalone.html` (everything in one file; accounts are not available there).

## Develop (VS Code + Claude Code)
`npm run serve` → http://localhost:5173 (guest mode only — no API) · `npm run build` → standalone file · `node tools/bump.mjs 2.14.0 "what changed"` → sets the version everywhere, writes `version.json` (what the in-app update check reads) and rebuilds `dist/`. To work on accounts and sync: `npx wrangler dev`, then `node tools/cloud-test.mjs` against it (never against the live site — it creates and deletes accounts). See **CLAUDE.md** for architecture, the local-dev recipe and conventions.

## Features
### Floor plan — step 1 of three (new in 2.14)
- **Three steps, in order.** The switch at the top is now **Plan · Wiring · Panel**: the plan draws the rooms, wiring fills one room with points and circuits, the panel builds the board those circuits come from. One project holds all three, and nothing stops you jumping about.
- **The whole floor from above.** Every room drawn with its walls, doors and windows, named, with its size and area. The points you place in the wiring step show as coloured dots, the board as a **DB** box, and the conduit can be laid over the plan too (*Cables*).
- **Move, resize, turn.** Drag a room to move it; pushed up against a neighbour it snaps so the two **share one wall** rather than ending up with two walls back to back. Drag the handle on a wall to resize, *Rotate* for a quarter turn, *Copy* to duplicate the room with everything in it.
- **Doors and windows.** Add them from the **+** library and drag them along a wall — or round a corner, where they simply re-home on the next wall. *Flip* swaps which side a door is hinged.
- **Straight on to the wiring.** *Wire this room →* opens that room in step 2. Rooms added in either step appear in both.
- **Dimensions** (each room's size, plus the overall extent), **Points** and **Cables** each toggle off, and the plan exports as a PNG or SVG drawing.
- Rooms in projects made before 2.14 are laid out in a row the first time you open the plan — drag them into place.

### Accounts, cloud sync and guest use (new in 2.13)
- **Use it as a guest, or sign in.** Never signing in changes nothing: designs live on the device exactly as before. **Menu → Account** (also offered once on first open) creates an account from a username and password — no e-mail needed.
- **Your projects follow you.** Signed in, every project is mirrored to the cloud in the background — a few seconds after an edit, and again whenever you open the app or come back to it. Use the same account on a phone and a laptop and they stay in step. The small dot before the project name shows where your latest edit stands: green = in the cloud, orange = waiting to upload, red = needs attention (open Menu → Account to see why).
- **It never makes you wait.** The copy on the device is the one you work on; the cloud catches up when there is signal and the app keeps working without it.
- **Edited on two devices?** Nothing is overwritten. Both versions are kept — the other one shows up as “<name> (cloud copy)” — and you pick what to keep.
- **History and Recently deleted.** Projects → History lists earlier versions the cloud kept while you worked and restores any of them as a new project. Account → Recently deleted brings back projects deleted in the last 90 days.
- **Signing out is safe.** It keeps your projects on the device; clearing the device is a separate, clearly worded choice that warns about anything that has not reached the cloud yet.
- **No password reset.** The password is stretched on your device and only the result is sent, so it cannot be recovered or reset — keep it somewhere safe. Every device you have used keeps its own copy of your projects, and **Back up everything** in Settings saves them to a file. Change password and Delete account are in Account.
- Needs an `https` page (the live site is); it is hidden in the offline one-file build.
- Not built yet: sharing a project with another account.


### Settings, version and updates (new in 2.12)
- **Menu → Settings** shows the app version, checks whether a newer one is out (**Check for updates**), and lists what changed. The app also checks on its own — shortly after opening, when you come back to it, and every couple of hours — and can be told not to.
- When an update exists, the menu button gets an **orange dot** and a small banner offers **Reload**. Your designs are saved on the device first; reloading never loses them.
- **Back up everything** writes every project to one file; **Import a file** adds them back (always as new projects — nothing is overwritten).
- **Protect from clearing** asks the browser not to evict saved designs when the device runs low on space.
- Preference changes apply immediately — there is no Save button.

### Room wiring (new in 2.1)
- **A room's DB is a panel** (2.10) — link a DB to one of the project's boards (DB → Edit → Panel) and it becomes that board: you design it in the panel editor, and its circuits are the ones the room can use. Put two DBs in a room on two different boards and each point runs back to the one that actually feeds it.
- **⚡ Circuits** — name, MCB rating and cable size. Circuits belong to the **board they come from**, so the same list appears in the panel editor and in every room fed by that board — created in one place, available in the other, with nothing to keep in sync.
- Every point picks its circuit from that list (Edit → Circuit). Unassigned is allowed.
- **Switching** (2.2) — a switch, switch board or fan regulator lists every light and fan in the room, one tick-list per gang, so you record what each gang actually drives. Loads are named with their position, so four identical downlights stay tellable apart.
- **Conduit runs** (2.3) — cables are routed along real conduit lines: a drop to a run height just under the ceiling, along the walls the shorter way round, then into the board; ceiling points cross the ceiling in two legs. Drawn in every wall / ceiling elevation and in 3D, the selected point's whole circuit picked out. Toggle with **Wiring**.
- **Wired the way it's installed** (2.7) — the board feeds switches and sockets with a **live feed (red)**, and a switch feeds its lights and fans with **switched live (yellow)**. So a light's cable comes from its switch, not the board, and at a glance you can see which lights are switched and which are fed direct. A light left on no circuit inherits its switch's.
- Cable per circuit and total conduit appear in the bill of materials.
- **Linked to the panel** (2.4) — a circuit can point at a real breaker on the board, and then shows that breaker's own rating instead of typed text. **Match by label** links every circuit whose name matches a breaker label in one tap. Flags a breaker used twice, and gives an indicative warning if the cable looks undersized for the breaker (a rough check, not a sizing calculation).

### Room designer — the Wiring step (new in 2.0)
- **Wiring** at the top edits one room: 4 walls + ceiling, size and wall names in ⚙ Room, and **🏠 Rooms** switches between the rooms drawn on the plan.
- Each wall opens as a flat elevation: drag points, snaps to 50 mm, standard heights (300 sockets, 1200 switches, 2100 AC…) and to other points; distance-to-corner and height dimensions; "All heights" mode.
- Ceiling plan with centre lines for fans and lights.
- Library: switches 1–4 gang, modular switch boards, fan regulator, AC isolator, 13A / 5A / 15A sockets, TV/data, wall light, exhaust fan, DB, junction boxes, downlights, LED panel, ceiling fan, pendant, smoke detector, doors and windows (cut into the wall in 3D).
- **Industrial (2.11)** — an **Industrial** tab plus industrial groups in Sockets and Lights: IEC 60309 sockets (1P/3P, 16–125 A, interlocked, IP44/IP67), weatherproof 13 A, enclosed isolators 1P–4P (20–125 A), emergency stops, push-button stations, contactors, DOL and star-delta starters, VFDs, motor / machine / pump / compressor / welding points, high bays, battens, flood lights, bulkheads, emergency lights and exit signs. Isolators, starters, contactors and E-stops **feed** what you assign to them, so board → isolator → starter → motor is drawn and measured leg by leg; load-side cable is orange. Circuits carry a phase (1φ / 3φ), and a 3-phase point on a single-phase way is flagged.
- 3D view: rotate / pinch / pan, walls toward you cut away, yellow labels (tap a label to select, again to open its wall), tap a wall to edit it.
- Export the current wall / ceiling drawing (PNG/SVG, with heights) or the 3D view (PNG with labels). Room points appear in the bill of materials.
- **Elevations sheet** (2.5) — Menu → Elevations sheet: all four walls and the ceiling on one page with a title block and a circuits table, as PNG or SVG.
- **Multiple rooms** (2.6) — 🏠 Rooms holds as many rooms as the job needs, all sharing the project's one panel. Switch, rename, duplicate (a full copy, links and all) or delete. The bill of materials covers every room.
### Panel designer
- Library: MCB 1P–4P, MCB 1P+N, RCD 2P/4P, RCBO, main switch, SPD, contactor, voltage relay, kWh meter 1φ/3φ, indicator lamp
- 35 mm DIN rails (6–48 modules) with module snapping and collision checks; enclosures
- **Panel size & fit** (2.8) — Menu → Panel size & fit: pick a generic enclosure, a typical consumer-unit size by way count, or type a custom W×H, and it tells you whether the design actually fits, naming anything outside the usable area and by how much, plus modules used vs free
- Earth/neutral bars, comb busbars, terminal blocks, incoming supply and outgoing cables
- Wiring: obstacle-avoiding orthogonal auto-routing with lane spreading; manual reshaping with multiple bends
- Layers: add/rename/reorder/delete, show/hide, lock, default layer for new parts and new wires, move parts or wires between layers
- Multi-select (Select more / Shift-click): move to layer, delete
- Undo/redo, multiple projects, JSON import/export, PNG/SVG export of visible layers
- **Multiple panels** (2.9) — Menu → Panels: as many boards as the job needs, each with its own devices, wiring and layers. Switch, rename, duplicate (wiring re-pointed at the copy) or delete. A room circuit can name a breaker on any panel in the project.
- Bill of materials with wire lengths (+15%) and rail usage, CSV export

## Changelog
- 2.14.0 — **The floor plan, and the work split into three steps.** The top switch is now **Plan · Wiring · Panel**, in the order you work: draw the rooms, wire a room, build its board. The new plan (`js/plan.js`) draws every room of the project from above — walls, doors and windows, each room named with its size and area, the wiring step's points as coloured dots, the board as a DB box, and optionally the conduit over the whole floor. Rooms are moved by dragging, resized by the handle on a wall and turned a quarter turn at a time; each room **keeps its own four walls**, so the plan adds only an `{x,z,rot}` per room and nothing in the wiring, the 3D view or any item coordinate had to change. Pushing one room against another snaps the gap between their inner faces to exactly one wall thickness, which puts both wall strips in the same place — so a shared partition draws as **one** wall, and a door in it shows from both sides (the floor is drawn in layers: all floors, then all walls, then all openings). Doors and windows are added from the **+** library and dragged along a wall or round a corner onto the next one, and *Flip* swaps a door's hinge. *Wire this room →* carries on into step 2; **🏠 Rooms** in the wiring step switches between the rooms on the plan. Dimensions, points and cables each toggle off, and the plan exports as PNG or SVG. Rooms in older projects are laid out in a row the first time the plan opens. Also fixed: duplicating a room lost every point's circuit — since 2.10 those ids live on the panel and were being cleared as if they were the room's
- 2.13.0 — **Accounts, cloud sync and guest use.** Designs no longer live only on one device. Menu → Account creates an account (username + password, no e-mail) and projects then sync to the cloud in the background; signed-out “guest” use is unchanged. The cloud is a **Cloudflare Worker with one SQLite Durable Object per account** (`worker/`, `wrangler.jsonc`) — the same deploy as the site, nothing extra to set up. The device stays the working copy and the app never waits on the network; a coloured dot before the project name shows green / orange / red for in the cloud / waiting / needs attention. Each project carries a revision, so a change made on two devices is never silently overwritten: both are kept (the other as “<name> (cloud copy)”). Deleting leaves a 90-day tombstone (**Account → Recently deleted** restores it) and the cloud keeps up to 40 earlier versions of each project (**Projects → History → Restore as a copy**). The password is stretched on the device (PBKDF2-SHA256, 300 000 rounds) and only the result is sent; sessions last 120 days, six wrong guesses lock the account for a growing delay, and **there is deliberately no password reset**. Change password, Delete account and a careful Sign out (keep or clear this device, warning about anything not yet uploaded) are in Account, which is also a section of Settings. The sample project made on first open is not uploaded until you change it, so a second device doesn't collect a duplicate. The Projects list now counts items across every panel rather than only the current one. Sync is hidden on `file://`, the one-file build, plain-`http` pages and plain static dev servers (they have no API); `tools/cloud-test.mjs` is a 63-check API test for `wrangler dev`. Optional `INVITE_CODE` variable restricts sign-ups
- 2.12.0 — **Settings, version and update check.** Menu → Settings now shows the app version and has **Check for updates**; the app also checks by itself (after opening, on coming back to the tab, every two hours) by reading a small `version.json` published with the site, and shows an orange dot on the menu button plus a Reload banner when something newer is out. There is deliberately no service worker, so nothing is cached behind your back — a reload fetches the new files. Settings also gained **Back up everything** / **Import a file** (a whole-device backup, restored as new projects so nothing is overwritten), a **Protect from clearing** button (asks the browser to keep saved designs), and a storage meter. Preferences apply as you change them. `tools/bump.mjs` sets the version everywhere, writes `version.json` and rebuilds `dist/` in one go
- 2.11.0 — **Industrial equipment, so a workshop can be wired.** 16 new point types: IEC 60309 industrial sockets (1P and 3P, 16–125 A, interlocked, IP44/IP67), weatherproof 13 A, enclosed isolators 1P–4P 20–125 A, emergency stops, push-button stations, contactors, DOL and star-delta starters, VFDs, motor and machine points, high bays, battens, flood lights, bulkheads, emergency lights and exit signs. **Control gear chains**: an isolator feeds a starter and the starter feeds the motor, each leg routed and measured, and a point left on no circuit inherits from whatever feeds it all the way up the chain. Load-side cable is drawn orange so it reads apart from the board's red feed and a switch's yellow switched live. Circuits gained a **phase** (1φ / 3φ) — cable is called out by core count in the bill of materials, and a 3-phase point sitting on a single-phase way is flagged. Cable sizes run to 95 mm², boards to 48 way. The panel library is unchanged and still domestic
- 2.10.1 — **Fix: importing a project exported by 2.9 or later failed.** The import check still required a top-level `items` array, which exports stopped having when panels became a list — so the app rejected its own backups, breaking the only way to move a project between devices. Both shapes are accepted now
- 2.10.0 — **A room's DB is a real panel.** Link a DB to one of the project's boards and it *is* that board — designed in the panel editor, and its circuits become the ones the room can use. Circuits moved from the room onto the panel that owns them, so there is one list shared by the panel editor and every room that board feeds, rather than two copies kept in step. With two DBs on two boards, each point routes back to the one that feeds it. Projects upgrade on load: circuits move onto the board, ids intact, and each room's DB is linked to it automatically
- 2.9.0 — **Multiple panels per project.** A project holds a list of boards, each with its own devices, wires and layers (Menu → Panels: add, switch, rename, duplicate, delete). Duplicating one re-ids every device and re-points all its wiring and rail references at the copy. Room circuits can now name a breaker on *any* panel, labelled with which board it is on. Projects from 2.8 and earlier — where the document itself was the panel — are wrapped into the list on load, unchanged
- 2.8.0 — **Panel size and a fit check.** The enclosure takes a custom W×H as well as presets, and the preset list now includes typical consumer-unit sizes by way count (4–24), worked from DIN module geometry. Menu → Panel size & fit reports whether the design fits the box, naming each part outside the usable area and by how much, with rail modules used vs free. Way-count sizes are a starting point to check a real enclosure against, not a manufacturer's spec
- 2.7.0 — **Wiring follows the real topology.** Cables no longer all run radially to the board: the DB feeds switches and sockets (live, red) and a switch feeds the lights and fans assigned to its gangs (switched live, yellow). A light with no circuit of its own inherits the circuit of the switch driving it; one not on any switch still gets a direct feed. Conductor colour is now meaning rather than decoration, so selecting a circuit changes line weight instead of hue, and the elevations sheet carries a key
- 2.6.0 — v3, part 3: **multiple rooms per project**. A project holds a list of rooms (🏠 Rooms — switch, add, rename, duplicate, delete) all sharing one panel, so a circuit in any room can point at its breakers. Duplicating a room deep-copies it with fresh ids and remaps its circuit and switch→load links onto the copy. The bill of materials covers every room, prefixed by room name. Single-room projects from 2.5 and earlier are wrapped into the list on load with nothing lost
- 2.5.0 — v3, part 2: **elevations sheet**. Each surface is captured by pointing the normal drawing code at it, so the sheet always matches what the editor shows; the editor’s own surface, zoom and selection are restored afterwards

A circuit can name a real MCB/RCBO/RCD on the board and then shows that device's actual rating rather than typed text; **Match by label** links everything whose circuit name matches a breaker label in one tap (fills blanks only, never double-books a breaker). Each assigned point is routed back to the DB along real conduit lines — up to a run height below the ceiling, around the walls the shorter way, down into the board — and drawn in the 2D elevations and in 3D, the selected point's circuit highlighted.
